GUIDE 02 — PROCLAMATION

Communicating the Gospel
in today’s context.

A generation is not arguing its way out of church. It is leaving quietly. This guide works out what the gospel actually is, how Jesus announced it, and what kind of messenger is worth listening to.

  • AuthorTariq Emmanuel
  • ThemeEvangelism and the next generation
  • ReadingAbout 7 minutes
  • OriginalCommunicating Gospel handout (PDF)
WHY THIS GUIDE EXISTS

The session behind this handout names its aim plainly: to end gospel poverty in the next generation—not for lack of churches, but for lack of clear, loving communication.

01

What the session sets out to do

Four learning outcomes

The handout opens with what a participant should be able to do by the end of the session.

  • Experience the world of the youth — understand the culture they actually live in.
  • Connect with youth — build genuine relationship rather than addressing a crowd.
  • Explore a variety of avenues — accept that one method will not reach everyone.
  • Recognise how youth can play an active role in church, and become a force bringing other youth to Christ.
02

What is the gospel?

Euangelion — good news

In the ancient world, euangelion meant good news: a message about something that has happened, announced to people who did not cause it and could not have arranged it.

The handout draws three sharp lines around the word:

  • The gospel is not your testimony. Your story is evidence of the gospel, not the gospel itself.
  • The gospel is not about what we have done for God. It is not advice, a moral programme, or a call to try harder.
  • The gospel is what God did for us. The good news is an announcement about God's action, not our achievement.
Creator God at last taking his power and reigning and putting things right. N. T. Wright, The Mission of the Church
The ultimate event of God's promise to rescue his creation has taken place in the life, death, resurrection, and ascension of the world's true King, Jesus. John Piper
}
03

The clearest summary in Scripture

1 Corinthians 15:1–5

When the handout asks which passage explains the gospel best, it answers with Paul's own summary—the message he says he passed on as of first importance.

Christ died for our sins according to the Scriptures, He was buried, and He was raised on the third day according to the Scriptures. 1 Corinthians 15:1–5

Four movements, and not one of them is something we do: died, buried, raised, and according to the Scriptures. That last phrase matters—this was God's plan all along, not an accident of history.

1 Corinthians 15:1–5
04

Jesus' first recorded message

Mark 1:14–15

The handout then goes to the beginning of Jesus' ministry, to the sentence he opened with.

The time is fulfilled, the kingdom of God has come near; repent and believe in the gospel. Mark 1:14–15
  • God's kingdom has come through Jesus. The reign of God is not a distant hope; it arrived in a person.
  • People must repent — turn from sin, not merely feel sorry for it.
  • And believe — trust in Him, not in our own record.

Repentance and faith are the two hands by which a person receives good news. Both are a response to something already done, never a way of earning it.

}
05

Group activity: find the gospel in the passage

Pick a reference and say what it tells us about the good news

In the live session, participants choose a reference and work out what it says about the gospel. Do the same now—read each one slowly before you decide how you would explain it to someone who has never opened a Bible.

  • John 3:16–17 — God's love through Jesus.
  • Romans 5:8 — Christ died while we were still sinners.
  • Jeremiah 29:11 — how to be saved.
  • Ephesians 2:8–9 — salvation is by grace, not works.

Then revisit the first definition of the gospel you ever wrote down, change anything you now think was thin, and share it with someone else in the room.

John 3:16–17 Romans 5:8 Jeremiah 29:11 Ephesians 2:8–9
06

Communicate effectively

Four qualities of a messenger who is heard

The handout is blunt about this: the message does not excuse the manner. Four words describe how the gospel is to be carried.

Clarity

Say what you mean in words an outsider can follow. Jargon is not reverence.

Listener

Know who is in front of you. Speak to them, not to your notes.

Empathy

Feel the weight of where they stand before you answer.

Respect

Honour the person, even when you disagree with the belief.

07

Why focus on the next generation?

Three commands to tell them

Scripture does not leave generational ministry as an option or a preference. It is framed as a duty handed from one generation to the next.

We will not hide them from their children; we will tell the next generation the praiseworthy deeds of the Lord, His power, and the wonders He has done. Psalm 78:4
One generation commends Your works to another; they tell of Your mighty acts. Psalm 145:4
And the things you have heard from me… entrust to reliable people who will also be qualified to teach others. 2 Timothy 2:2
Psalm 78:4 Psalm 145:4 2 Timothy 2:2
08

Knowing the generations by year

The handout's own table

The session lays out the generational cohorts so a leader can locate the people in front of them.

  • Greatest Generation (1901–1927) — fought in the Second World War.
  • Silent Generation (1928–1945) — grew up during the Great Depression and the war.
  • Baby Boomers (1946–1964) — the post-war baby boom.
  • Generation X (1965–1980) — economic uncertainty and change.
  • Millennials / Gen Y (1981–1996) — digital-age natives.
  • Generation Z (1997–2012) — the first true digital natives.
  • Generation Alpha (2013–2025) — tech and global natives.

Gen Z (1997–2012) is the focus of this session. At the time the handout was written, that cohort ranged from about fourteen to twenty-nine years old—youth group and young adult ministry at the same time.

}
09

Fifteen minutes of silence

The exercise that lands hardest

The session stops for fifteen minutes of silence, and then says this:

That silence represents what is happening in many churches today: Gen Z is not arguing, not debating… they are just leaving quietly. Tariq Emmanuel, Communicating the Gospel in Today's Context

They are not usually lost to a rival argument. They drift. That is why the handout ends where it began: not with a technique, but with prayer.

Let us pray to reach them and save them for Christ. To end gospel poverty.

}
10

Put it to work this week

Three ways to carry this guide

  1. Write your own one-sentence answer to “What is the gospel?” using only the four movements of 1 Corinthians 15:3–4. No jargon, no testimony—just the announcement.
  2. Take one of the four qualities above—clarity, listener, empathy, respect—and ask someone who knows you well which one is your weakest. Then work on it deliberately.
  3. Open the goal planner and set one goal for the young people already in your life, and put their names on your prayer wall.
