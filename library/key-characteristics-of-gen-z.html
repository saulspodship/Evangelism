GUIDE 05 — UNDERSTANDING

Key characteristics
of Gen Z.

Know, so you can reach. Six strengths worth thanking God for, six challenges worth shepherding, and a prayer wall for carrying a generation before the Lord.

  • AuthorTariq Emmanuel
  • CohortBorn 1997–2012
  • ReadingAbout 6 minutes
  • OriginalKey Characteristics handout (PDF)
START WITH A PRAYER WALL

The handout opens with an activity, not a lecture. Each participant writes one concern they have for Gen Z, pins it to a prayer wall, and then the group prays through every request together.

Do that now in the prayer wall tool—write the first concern that comes to mind, and add to it as you read.

01

Six strengths to celebrate

“Say loud: one positive quality of Gen Z that we can thank God for.”

The session insists on starting with gratitude rather than diagnosis. Before a single weakness is named, here is what is genuinely good about this generation.

Digital natives

They grew up with smartphones, tablets, social media, and the internet. They are very comfortable with technology and quick to learn new digital tools.

Visual and fast learners

They prefer video, short content, and TikTok-style communication. They respond well to visuals, interactive media, and quick information.

They value authenticity

They want real, honest communication. They dislike fake or overly polished content.

Socially aware

Gen Z often cares about social issues—justice, equality, the environment. They respond when leaders stand for something meaningful.

Independent and creative

They express identity through fashion, music, and content creation. Many explore entrepreneurship early.

A desire for purpose

They look for meaning and purpose in studies, work, and faith. Messages that connect to real life and personal growth impact them.

Do the exercise: say out loud—or write down—one positive quality of Gen Z that you can thank God for and celebrate. Not a polite compliment; something true.

}
02

Six challenges to shepherd

Know, so you can reach

Only after the thanks does the handout turn to the struggles. Read these as pastoral needs, not as accusations.

Challenge 1

Identity confusion

Gen Z is constantly asking: “Who am I?” “Where do I belong?” “Am I valuable?”

They face social media comparison, pressure to create a perfect online identity, and confusion about purpose and identity in a fast-changing world.

Spiritual issue

They do not know who they are in Christ.

Challenge 2

Loneliness and mental-health struggles

Even though they are digitally connected, many Gen Z feel isolated, anxious, and depressed.

The handout's diagnosis is simple and uncomfortable: they replace real relationships with screens.

Spiritual issue

Connection without relationship leaves the soul alone.

Challenge 3

Overload of information, lack of truth

Gen Z is exposed to thousands of voices, endless opinions, and relativism—“your truth / my truth.”

Spiritual issue

They struggle to identify absolute truth.

Challenge 4

Distrust of institutions

Gen Z often mistrust churches, leaders, and organised religion. They want authenticity, not traditional structures.

Spiritual issue

Trust must be earned through transparency, not demanded by position.

Challenge 5

Short attention span

Because of TikTok and reels, messages must be short, clear, visual, and memorable. Long sermons without engagement lose them quickly.

Spiritual issue

The message does not change; the doorway into it must be narrower.

Challenge 6

Fear of commitment

They hesitate to commit to church, stick with a ministry, or make long-term decisions. They want flexibility and freedom.

Spiritual issue

Discipleship is learned through staying, so this is the deepest pastoral need.

}
03

The line worth memorising

What they need instead of criticism

Gen Z does not need more criticism. They need shepherds. Tariq Emmanuel, Key Characteristics of Gen Z

Everything in this guide points the same direction. A shepherd knows the sheep, walks ahead of them, and stays when it is inconvenient. That is why this library pairs this page with Biblical Mentoring.

}
04

Mission practice

Complete a personal list to reach Gen Z

The handout ends with an assignment: complete a personal list of the Gen Z people you will reach. Not a strategy for a group—a list of names.

  1. Write between three and ten names of Gen Z people you already know.
  2. Beside each name, note one thing they are carrying from the challenges above.
  3. Beside each name, note one strength in them worth thanking God for.
  4. Put every name on your prayer wall, and set one goal in the planner for the next ninety days.
