GUIDE 03 — CORRECTING MYTHS

Five major myths
about youth.

Every church has a sentence it repeats about young people. Five of the most common ones are false—and each has Scripture to answer it and a step to take this month.

  • AuthorTariq Emmanuel
  • ThemeYouth in the church
  • ReadingAbout 5 minutes
  • OriginalFive Major Myths handout (PDF)
READ THIS ONE IN A ROOM

Five sentences get repeated about young people in almost every church. Every one of them is a myth, and every one has a biblical answer and a practical solution attached. Read them slowly—and notice which one you have said out loud.

01

Five myths and the truth that answers them

Each myth with its Scripture and its solution

Myth 1

“Youth are not ready for serious spiritual responsibility.”

Biblical truth

God has always used young people in powerful ways.

  • Samuel heard God's voice as a child (1 Samuel 3).
  • David was chosen and anointed as a youth (1 Samuel 16).
  • Jeremiah said, “I am too young,” but God replied, “Do not say, ‘I am too young’… I am with you” (Jeremiah 1:6–8).
  • Timothy was young, yet Paul trusted him to lead churches (1 Timothy 4:12).

The solution

Give youth real ministry roles—worship, tech, teaching moments, missions, small groups—not just entertainment.

1 Samuel 3 1 Samuel 16 Jeremiah 1:6–8 1 Timothy 4:12
Myth 2

“Youth only want entertainment, not discipleship.”

Biblical truth

Jesus called young disciples to a radical, costly path.

  • Jesus called his followers to take up their cross daily and follow Him (Luke 9:23).
  • The early disciples were likely teenagers and young adults.
  • They followed Jesus not because of entertainment, but because of truth, purpose, and calling.

The solution

Teach the Bible deeply, honestly, and practically. Give youth a mission, not a show.

Luke 9:23
Myth 3

“Youth are naturally rebellious and difficult to guide.”

Biblical truth

Youth are responsive to love, patience, and shepherding.

  • Jesus welcomed children and youth: “Let the little children come to me” (Matthew 19:14).
  • Paul instructed leaders to correct gently, not harshly (2 Timothy 2:24–25).
  • Proverbs teaches guidance through relationship, not force (Proverbs 22:6).

The solution

Invest in mentoring relationships, not just preaching. Speak with youth, not at youth.

Matthew 19:14 2 Timothy 2:24–25 Proverbs 22:6
Myth 4

“Youth don’t care about the church or God.”

Biblical truth

Young people deeply desire purpose, identity, and truth.

  • Solomon says: “Remember your Creator in the days of your youth” (Ecclesiastes 12:1).
  • Timothy served faithfully from childhood (2 Timothy 3:14–15).
  • Young people are often the first responders to God's call (Matthew 4:18–22).

The solution

Show youth that faith is relevant, alive, meaningful, and connected to their world. Help them discover their identity in Christ, not culture.

Ecclesiastes 12:1 2 Timothy 3:14–15 Matthew 4:18–22
Myth 5

“Youth are too influenced by culture and technology to grow spiritually.”

Biblical truth

God's truth can renew minds regardless of culture.

  • Scripture teaches that minds are renewed rather than merely restrained (Romans 12:2).
  • Daniel, a teenager in Babylon, remained faithful in a pagan culture (Daniel 1:8).
  • Joseph served God faithfully in Egypt as a young man.
  • God's Word is powerful in every generation (Psalm 119:9).

The solution

Teach youth how to use technology for God's glory: Bible apps, Christian music, devotionals, healthy digital boundaries, and accountability.

Romans 12:2 Daniel 1:8 Psalm 119:9
02

The pattern in all five

What every myth has in common

Look at the five together and a shape appears. Every myth assumes the problem is the young person. Every answer assumes the problem is the distance—and moves an adult closer.

Myth says: not ready

Answer: give them something real to carry.

Myth says: only want fun

Answer: give them a mission worth the cost.

Myth says: rebellious

Answer: shepherd instead of controlling.

Myth says: they don't care

Answer: show them a faith that is actually alive.

Myth says: culture is too strong

Answer: teach them to use it for God's glory.

03

Audit your own church

Questions to ask honestly

  1. Name the last three ministry responsibilities you gave to someone under twenty-five. Were they real, or were they holding a microphone someone else was operating?
  2. When did a young person last ask you a hard question and get an honest answer rather than a correction?
  3. Does your church have a route from attending to leading, or does leadership simply take a long time to arrive?
  4. What would change this month if you believed myth one was false?

Write your answers into the goal planner under Ministry, and put the names of the young people you named on your prayer wall.
